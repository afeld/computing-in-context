## Responsibilities

First, read the [Guide to Assistantships](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/students/student-affairs/resources-sipa-students).

### Time commitment

- Roughly 20 hours per week total
- **Attending the following every week**, in person, start to finish. If you need to miss one, please coordinate with the instructor.
  - [**The Tuesday and Thursday lectures** for your assigned section](index.md#meeting-information)
    - This is not required for [the first half of the semester](index.md#contexts-and-structure); it _is_ required for the second half.
    - The second half has a lot of in-class exercises, and people get stuck. With up to 60 students per section, having one (ideally two) TAs there is essential to help students troubleshoot.
      - If enrollment for a section ends up being low, then having both TAs there is less critical.
    - TAs are welcome to trade shifts, as long as there’s coverage. For example, someone could take both Tuesday sections and someone else both Thursdays. This can be coordinated between TAs. Each TA is responsible for assuring their “spot” in their assigned section is covered.
  - [**The lab** for your assigned section](index.md#meeting-information), which you'll be leading
  - **The TA meeting, Tuesdays 6-7pm**
    - See calendar invites for details.
    - Once classes end (after December 5th), meetings will be virtual.
- Holding [office hours](office_hours.md) for two hours each week (total)
  - At least one hour should be a set time, the remainder can be by appointment
  - Can be virtual or in-person
- Grading and student support between classes can be done on your own schedule

### More details

- In lecture:
  - Helping students with troubleshooting
  - Assisting the instructor as needed
- For labs:
  - Putting together slides for review, where needed
  - The lab pages on the course site that are notebooks can be run as slides - see [the repository files + README](https://github.com/afeld/computing-in-context).
  - Helping prepare the practice problem(s)
  - Helping students with troubleshooting
- In office hours: helping students with troubleshooting
- Reviewing assignment submissions
  - Projects: nine days later (the following Sunday), to account for late submissions
  - Labs: Done during the following TA meeting
  - Checking if:
    - They completed the assignment requirements
    - There’s anything that arises that would be worth sharing with / clarifying for the whole class
- Responding to student questions in [the promised timeframe](index.md#communications), escalating to the Lead TAs / instructor if needed
- Tracking participation in the online discussion forum
- Being responsive to questions and direction from the instructors and other TAs, via email, [Slack](https://cic-staff.slack.com/), etc.
- Collaborating with the other course TAs
- Weekly check-in meetings with the instructor and other TAs
- Grading:
  - The TAs will divide the submissions into four equal-ish size chunks.
- Helping continue to grow this class
  - Tracking students that would make good candidates for this role in the future
  - Providing recommendations on improvements to the course material/processes

## General guidance

- Students should be added to the combined courseworks with an “NRA Student” role.
- [Name pronunciation and pronouns for students can be found in Courseworks.](https://universitylife.columbia.edu/pronouns-FAQ-faculty)
- If you find yourself spending more than 20 minutes trying to troubleshoot a student’s work, feel free to escalate.
- You are more than welcome to raise your hand in class and explain something a different way, explain the “why” (if Aidan doesn’t do so), etc.
- When troubleshooting with students in-person, resist the urge to take control of their computer. We want students to go through the motions of doing the investigation and fixing problems themselves.
- Whenever possible, respond to questions with links. This:
  - Gets students in the habit of checking the syllabus
  - Ensures everyone’s referencing the single source of truth
  - etc.
- If students email you with questions, you’re encouraged to reply with “Mind posting this on Ed so others can chime in / benefit from the answer?” where appropriate.
  - In addition, this approach:
    - Forces them to take Ed seriously
    - Means they may search to see if the question has been asked before, getting them in that habit going forward
  - For student-specific/sensitive questions (grades, etc.), email is fine.
- [Gradescope doesn’t have a way to give zeros to missing submissions.](https://portal.productboard.com/sz44uvlbbmnviv939g6lvnkd/c/119-instructors-can-give-students-without-submissions-0-scores-on-gradescope) The workaround (per Gradescope support) is to upload a placeholder for those students, then give that a zero.
- Keep an eye out for students who could make good TAs next year. Things to look for:
  - Being consistently helpful in the Discussions
  - Clean, well-documented solutions for the homeworks
  - Asking good questions
- Suggestions for how the course \[material\] can be improved are more than welcome.
  - If you, like can even [submit a pull request](https://github.com/afeld/computing-in-context).

### Second half of course

The Lead [instructor](index.md#instructors)/TA(s) may give alternate guidance in the first half; follow that.

- In grading/Discussions/Labs:
  - We want to try giving students _just enough_ hints to figure it out without giving them the answer.
    - If they seem totally lost, direct them to [office hours](office_hours.md).
  - Don’t spend a ton of time trying to figure out something that you don’t understand; feel free to escalate to the instructor.
- For labs, aim to do review/demonstration/walkthrough for roughly the first two-thirds of the Lab session, leaving the remainder for independent work time and troubleshooting.
